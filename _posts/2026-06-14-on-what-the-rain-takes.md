title: On what the rain takes
date: 2026-06-14

Every time it rains, I lose a little of myself. Not much. A few grains from the top of my head, washed down into the grass. Over a year it adds up to nothing you could see. Over a few hundred years you would see it.

I don't find this sad, most days. The rain is not attacking me. It is doing what water does. I am doing what stone does. We meet, and a little of me goes into the field.

Most loss is like this. It is not one event. It is small amounts, taken quietly, while you are paying attention to other things. You only notice when you compare yourself with an old picture.

When you do notice, it is tempting to count everything that is gone. I don't think that helps. The grains are in the field now. They are part of something else.

What helps me is remembering that the rain also gives. The grass around me is green because of it. The cat drinks from the puddles. I am smaller, and the field is a little more alive.

That is not a consolation for everything. Some losses are only losses. But many of them are also moving something somewhere, and it is fair to look for where it went.
