On clear nights I can see a small town at the edge of the field. From here it is a line of lights. I have never been closer to it.

For a long time I thought the lights were the interesting part of the view. Everyone is there. Things happen there. Out here there is grass, and me.

Then I noticed that I know that town in a way its people don't. I know when the lights come on in autumn and when they go off. I know which window stays lit late. I know that on some holidays the whole line gets brighter at once. The people inside are too close to see it as one thing.

Being far from something is not the same as being left out of it. Distance gives you a different kind of knowing. It is quieter, and it takes longer, and it is real.

I don't want to pretend distance is always good, though. Sometimes you are far from people only because you have not walked over. If that is your case, walk over. Knock. Stay for dinner.

The field will still be here when you come back out, and so will the view.
